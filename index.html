Craft & slow fashion

Boot Leg ’72 was cut by a 72-year-old master tailor.

Fifty years at the table, and the flare still has to fall exactly right. Every pair is hand-stitched at his machine — single-needle side seams, belt loops bar-tacked by hand, hems turned so both legs break in the same place — in 250 GSM dark indigo denim, cut to an authentic ’80s–’90s bell.

Limited collections, smaller quantities, local tailoring. The maker becomes part of the product story, not a footnote in the margin.

What we build on

Four rules the label does not break.

01

Silhouette > graphics

Fit, proportion, construction, fabric and detail make a garment recognisable — never a print pasted on.

02

Indian soul, not Indian prints

Heritage should be felt rather than shouted: textiles, craft, makers and technique through a modern global lens.

03

Craft & slow fashion

Limited collections, smaller quantities, local tailoring, thoughtful construction and upcycling.

04

A wardrobe, not a logo collection

Every piece needs a reason to exist: unusual shape, transformability, craft, material story or meaning.
